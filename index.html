The problem

Camera plans are drawn in 2D. Blind spots show up after installation.

The building

Walls, columns, glazing and ceiling height decide what a camera can actually see.

The camera

Mounting height, tilt and lens decide how many pixels land on a person.

The requirement

Detecting a silhouette and identifying a face need very different detail. Most tools check a theoretical field of view, one camera at a time.

Moving a camera once it is installed means a new bracket, new cabling and another site visit. VanGuard finds the problem on the plan.

VanGuard Available now

From the CAD plan to a coverage audit you can defend.

VanGuard reads the architect’s DXF plan and the integrator’s camera layout, builds the building in 3D, and measures room by room what each camera sees at its required DORI level. It then proposes settings your brackets can reach and writes the reports for decision-makers and installers.

  1. 01

    Import the plan

    Drop the architect’s DXF. Units, layers and camera symbols are recognised, and anything unreadable is flagged before you go further.

    DXF · camera layout
  2. 02

    Build the 3D building

    Walls, openings, columns, glazing and ceilings are generated automatically. Walk through it at eye level.

    Generated in 1–2 min
  3. 03

    Measure real coverage

    Every square metre is tested against every camera: covered, seen by several cameras, or blind, at each camera’s DORI level.

    EN 62676-4
  4. 04

    Optimise and export

    A proposed height, tilt and focal length for each camera, within what its bracket allows. Then a printable report for decision-makers and a camera-by-camera schedule for installers.

    Report · schedule · CSV

The required detail, in metres

DORI sets how many pixels per metre must land on a person. VanGuard uses the same method as market calculators, so an integrator who re-runs the numbers finds the same distances.

Example: 4K dome camera, 3,840 px sensor, 112° field of view at 3.6 mm.

Standard method

DORI ranges follow EN 62676-4, the method used by market calculators.

Every assumption stated

Mounting heights, glazing, doors and ceiling heights are written on screen and in every report.

Nothing hidden in the total

Rooms without a camera and excluded areas are listed with their surface.

Settings you can install

No camera added or moved, no tilt beyond the bracket’s travel, no covered area lost.

Sentinel In development

Sentinel runs the same audit on the real site.

A plan shows the building as designed. Sentinel captures it as built, renders the image each camera will really deliver and tests your detection AI through every view.

  1. Capture the site

    Photos or a video are enough. Gaussian Splatting turns them into a navigable, photorealistic twin.

  2. Simulate every camera

    Lens distortion, field of view and resolution, from measured camera models.

  3. Test the detection AI

    Synthetic people are placed in the scene and your detector is scored through each simulated camera.
